The Fellowship

Meltem is a guided, self-directed fellowship for people who want to explore art more seriously, both by looking and by making. It is fully remote, so you can take part from wherever you are, and it adapts to your life.

Piano a piano.

How it works

  1. Flagship project. A personal project with a clear goal, ideally connected to an art competition or open call.
  2. Collaborations. Work with the mentor, other fellows or other creatives.
  3. Meetings. Regular check-ins with your mentor, at least 30 minutes a month, and more whenever you would like them.
  4. Graduation project. A closing piece that marks the end of your fellowship. Collaboration time counts towards your practice hours.

Discovery and practice

Learning log. You record each piece of art you engage with: what it was, how long you spent, its type, your group, the context, the technique and your analysis, and how it made you feel.

Practice log. You record the time you spend on your own projects and collaborations. Activities that sit between art and other fields can count too, at a rate agreed with your mentor.

A statistics page shows your total, and how your time was shared across types and groups.

Time commitment. Flexible, with a minimum of 100 hours: at least 50 of learning and at least 50 of practice, plus mentor meetings.

What you receive. A certificate of completion, an overview of your performance, a reference letter, guidance and feedback, a line on your CV and LinkedIn, and a way to make the time you give to art visible.

Questions, answered

Is the fellowship paid?

No. It is unpaid, and there are no fees either. We would rather be completely clear about this from the start. What you receive is guidance, structure and recognition of the work you do.

Is it an internship or a degree?

Neither. It is an independent, mentored programme. When you complete it, you receive a certificate of completion and a reference letter, but not an academic qualification.

Is it really fully remote?

Yes. Everything happens online, so you can join from anywhere. We will find meeting times that suit your time zone.

Do I need a background in art?

No formal training is needed. Curiosity and a willingness to practise matter most.

How long does it last?

It is flexible and follows your availability, with a minimum of 100 hours. Some people will take a few weeks, others a few months, and both are fine.

How are hours counted?

Through a shared spreadsheet. You report your learning and practice time.

What is a flagship project?

A project with a clear goal, such as a piece for a competition or open call. If it is accepted, published or exhibited, that is wonderful, but the real value lies in the process.

What kind of feedback will I receive?

Honest, specific and kind. We will talk about your ideas as well as your technique.

Who owns my work?

You do, always.
